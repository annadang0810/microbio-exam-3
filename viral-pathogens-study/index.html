The Big Picture

Lecture 24 contrasts two very different viruses your professor explicitly flagged for the exam. Both invade neurons, but their genomes, life cycles, and clinical strategies are opposites.

  • Herpesvirus — enveloped, dsDNA, replicates in the nucleus, establishes lifelong latency in sensory neurons. No cure, no vaccine.
  • Poliovirus — non-enveloped, +ssRNA, replicates in the cytoplasm, causes acute, often fatal paralysis. Two vaccines, near-eradicated.

Herpesviridae Family Members & Diseases HIGH YIELD

Why Herpes is Special (set the scene)

  • Hominids have carried Herpesviruses for an estimated 8 million years
  • Plagued the ancient Greeks, who named it herpein — "to creep"
  • Humans are the only reservoir
  • Infections persist for life — cause recurrent infection in 1/3 of the world's population
  • No cure, no vaccine for herpes

The Five Members You Must Know HIGH YIELD

VirusDisease(s)
HSV-1 (Herpes Simplex Virus 1)Oral herpes — cold sores
HSV-2 (Herpes Simplex Virus 2)Genital herpes
Varicella-Zoster Virus (VZV)Chickenpox (primary) and shingles (reactivation)
Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV)Mononucleosis ("mono")
Cytomegalovirus (CMV)Mononucleosis (CMV mono)

Two Critical Facts About the Family

  • More than 90% of adults have been infected with at least one of these viruses
  • The defining trait of every member of Herpesviridae is the ability to establish latent infection

HSV-1 / HSV-2 Structure

  • Large icosahedral capsid wrapped in a phospholipid envelope (with a tegument layer between)
  • Linear, double-stranded DNA genome encoding ~70 genes
  • HSV-1 and HSV-2 genomes share ~70% identity — the lecture refers to them collectively as HSV
  • Relatively fragile outside the body — depend on intimate contact between mucous membranes to spread
Memory hook: "H S V V E C" — HSV-1, HSV-2, VZV, EBV, CMV. All Herpesviridae. All establish latency. Most people carry at least one.

HSV Mechanism of ENTRY HIGH YIELD

The model for HSV entry was derived from cryo-electron microscopy of cells trapped at different infection stages. It's a glycoprotein-driven, three-step process.

The 3 Glycoproteins You Need

  • gB — first contact; associates with a host receptor
  • gD — binds a different host receptor and triggers a conformational change
  • gH/gL (the fusion complex) — recruited after gD's conformational change; mediates membrane fusion

Step-by-Step Entry HIGH YIELD

  1. Attachment via gB — viral glycoprotein gB associates with a host receptor
  2. gD triggers a switch — gD binds a DIFFERENT host receptor and undergoes a conformational change
  3. Fusion via gH/gL — the fusion complex (gH/L) is recruited; it mediates the merging of the lipid bilayers (viral envelope ↔ host plasma membrane) and the release of the nucleocapsid into the host cytoplasm
HSV entry sequence: [ virion ] gB → host receptor #1 (attachment) gD → host receptor #2 ⇒ conformational change gH/gL recruited ⇒ lipid bilayer fusion ⇒ nucleocapsid released into cytoplasm
The mnemonic: gB attaches, gD switches, gH/L fuses. Three glycoproteins, three jobs — in that order.

HSV TRANSIT — Hijacking Microtubule Motors HIGH YIELD

Why HSV Needs to Travel

  • HSV preferentially invades sensory neurons
  • It must transit the long distance between the neuronal axon (entry site) and the nucleus (replication site) to complete its life cycle
  • It has to make this trip in BOTH directions: in to establish latency, out for shedding

The Cellular Highway: Microtubules

  • The cell's transportation system is built from tubulin, organized via microtubule-organizing centers (MTOCs)
  • This system normally carries cellular cargo (e.g., vesicles) between the cell periphery and the nucleus
  • Microtubules are polar — they have a plus end (typically at the cell periphery) and a minus end (at the MTOC, near the nucleus)

The Two Motor Proteins (memorize the directions!) HIGH YIELD

MotorDirectionFunctional meaning
Dynein (blue)Toward the MINUS endToward the MTOC / nucleus — INWARD transport
Kinesin (beige)Toward the PLUS endToward the nucleus per slide... but in neurons the plus ends point to axon terminals, so kinesin = OUTBOUND egress

Note from lecture: Both proteins "walk" along microtubules, hand-over-hand. Dynein takes cargo TO the MTOC; kinesin takes cargo AWAY from it (toward the cell periphery / axon terminal).

HSV Hitches a Ride

  • HSV nucleocapsids hitch a ride on dynein and kinesin!
  • This is required for transit to and from the nucleus
  • Live-cell imaging with fluorescent HSV capsids visualizes this transport in real time
  • Many viruses have evolved to bind directly to host motor proteins — an example of viral coevolution with host machinery

Other Viruses That Exploit These Motors (lecture table)

Use DYNEIN to enterUse KINESIN for egress
HSV-1, CMV, KSHV, HIV-1, HPV, Adenovirus, Parvovirus, Influenza AKSHV, VSV, HIV-1, Sendai virus, African swine fever virus, Vaccinia
The HSV transit story: nucleocapsids dock on dynein for the trip IN to the nucleus, and on kinesin for the trip OUT after replication. The virus doesn't build its own transport; it just gets on the host's bus system.

HSV SILENCING & LATENCY HIGH YIELD

Step 1: How the Innate Immune System Tries to Silence HSV

  • Innate immune sensor IFI16 patrols the nucleus for foreign DNA
  • IFI16 packages naked foreign DNA into tight balls of chromatin
  • This limits access to the host transcriptional machinery — the viral genome can't be read
  • It also triggers proinflammatory cytokine production
  • End result: HSV is effectively silenced

Step 2: HSV Counteracts Silencing in Epithelial Cells (LYTIC cycle)

HSV evolved to fight silencing in epithelial cells. Three weapons:

  • One viral protein degrades surveillance system components
  • A second set of viral proteins keeps viral promoters free of repressive chromatin (the lecture highlights VP16 and ICP0 driving active marks like H3K4me2 and acetylation)
  • Other viral proteins modify host RNA polymerase to direct it toward viral promoters

Together these factors orchestrate the HSV lytic cycle, causing the cold sores and lesions characteristic of HSV.

Step 3: HSV ENCOURAGES Silencing in Neurons (LATENCY) HIGH YIELD

This is the twist. In neurons, HSV does the OPPOSITE of what it does in epithelial cells:

  • Within neuronal cells, HSV does NOT subvert silencing
  • Instead, HSV actively recruits chromatin assembly — the viral genome is packaged into heterochromatin marked by H3K9me3
  • The result: only ONE HSV gene product is highly expressed — the LAT (Latency-Associated Transcript)
  • LAT is a viral RNA that does two key things:
    • Promotes tight packaging of the viral genome
    • Prevents apoptosis of the infected neuron — keeps the host cell alive long-term

So for the duration of its long life, the neuron harbors a silent HSV genome waiting to be reactivated.

SAME virus, OPPOSITE behavior in two cell types: EPITHELIAL CELL: HSV FIGHTS silencing → LYTIC cycle (cold sores, lesions) active chromatin marks (H3K4me2, Ac) ICP0 + VP16 + RNA Pol II hijack NEURONAL CELL: HSV PROMOTES silencing → LATENT infection heterochromatin marks (H3K9me3) Only LAT expressed → tight packaging + anti-apoptosis
The exam-ready summary: IFI16 silences foreign DNA via chromatin; HSV beats this in epithelial cells (lytic) but EMBRACES it in neurons (latent), where LAT is the only transcript and it both packs the genome and keeps the neuron alive.

HSV REACTIVATION HIGH YIELD

What Triggers It

HSV can reactivate when the host cell senses stress:

  • Emotional stress
  • Physical stress
  • Clinical stress (e.g., illness, immunosuppression)
  • Age-associated immune decline

The Full Cycle: Latency → Reactivation → Shedding

A. Establishment of latency (primary infection): Epithelial cell —PRIMARY INFECTION→ sensory Neuron ↓ LATENCY (LAT only, H3K9me3) B. Reactivation: Neuron —STRESS / IMMUNE DECLINE→ REACTIVATION ↓ Nucleocapsids deactivate chromatin silencing ↓ Viral genes expressed → replication restarts ↓ Travel back to axonal surface via microtubules ↓ Shed virions infect neighboring epithelial cells ↓ New round of LYTIC infection → cold sores / lesions

The Mechanism Step-by-Step

  1. Silencing reversed: in response to stress, nucleocapsids/host factors deactivate chromatin silencing on the latent HSV genome
  2. Replication resumes: viral gene expression restarts and the replication cycle begins
  3. Outbound transport: new nucleocapsids travel back to the axonal surface via the microtubule network (kinesin)
  4. Shedding: virions are shed at the periphery
  5. Lytic re-infection: shed virions infect neighboring epithelial cells, initiating a new round of LYTIC infection — the visible cold sore / lesion

Therapeutics: Acyclovir

  • HSV-2 is the primary causative agent of genital herpes; ~50 million Americans are infected
  • There is no cure, but antivirals can manage flares
  • Acyclovir is the most commonly prescribed antiviral; given during flare-ups
  • Mechanism: it's a guanosine BASE ANALOG — missing the normal ribose ring (acyclic side chain instead)
  • It completely inactivates HSV-specific DNA polymerases and prevents further viral DNA synthesis
  • Crucially, acyclovir has much higher affinity for VIRAL DNA polymerase than host DNAP — so it doesn't hurt the host
Full HSV cycle for the exam: Entry (gB → gD → gH/L fusion) → Transit IN (dynein) → Silencing (LAT only, H3K9me3, no apoptosis) → Reactivation under stress → Transit OUT (kinesin) → Shed → Lytic cycle in epithelium → Cold sore.

Polio: Pathology & Epidemiology HIGH YIELD

Epidemiology

  • Polio (poliomyelitis) is caused by the highly contagious poliovirus
  • Worldwide distribution — year-round in the tropics, summer/fall in temperate zones
  • Affects all age groups, but children are more susceptible than adults
  • Once acquired, immunity is life-long
  • Humans are the only known reservoir → eradication is biologically possible
  • Two major vaccines available (IPV, OPV) → eradication has been pursued aggressively
  • Cases dropped from 350,000 in 1988 (125 countries) to 1,292 in 2010 (4 endemic countries)

Three Clinical Outcomes HIGH YIELD

OutcomeFrequencySymptoms
Asymptomatic~95%No symptoms; person still sheds virus
Abortive polio (minor illness)~5%Fever, malaise, sore throat, myalgia, headache
Paralytic polio (major illness)~0.1%Asymmetric flaccid paralysis of lower or upper extremities, chest, or abdomen

Severity of Paralytic Polio

  • Paralytic polio with respiratory involvement is fatal without respiratory support (the iconic "iron lung")
  • Case fatality rate for paralytic polio: 5-10%
  • Post-polio syndrome: progressive muscle atrophy years later, even after initial recovery

How Poliovirus Reaches the CNS

Polio is fundamentally a gut virus that becomes a nervous system virus:

  • Once poliovirus enters the bloodstream, it can be circulated to the central nervous system
  • Spreads along axons of peripheral nerves to the CNS
  • Spreads along fibers of the lower motor neurons to the spinal cord or brain
  • Invades certain types of nerve cells — damages or destroys them as a result of replication
  • Destruction of nerve cells in the spinal cord causes irreversible muscle wasting and paralysis (because lower motor neurons control muscle, and they don't regenerate)
Memorize the percentages: 95% asymptomatic / 5% abortive / 0.1% paralytic, with 5-10% case fatality once paralytic. The pathology is irreversible because polio kills lower motor neurons in the spinal cord.

Poliovirus Structure & Replication HIGH YIELD

Structure

  • Human enterovirus — mouth is the portal of entry, primary multiplication occurs in the gastrointestinal tract
  • Family: Picornaviridae ("pico" = small + RNA)
  • Genome: single-stranded, POSITIVE-sense RNA (+ssRNA), ~7,500 nucleotides
  • Capsid: icosahedral symmetry, ~30 nm in diameter
  • NON-enveloped (naked virion → resists stomach acid → can be fecal-orally transmitted)

Why "Positive-Sense RNA" Matters

+RNA can be directly translated by host ribosomes as if it were an mRNA — no need for the virus to bring an RNA polymerase into the cell first. This makes the early steps simple and fast.

The 8-Step Replication Cycle HIGH YIELD

  1. Binding: attachment to the cell-surface receptor CD155 on intestinal epithelial cells
  2. Entry by endocytosis: the virion is taken up via endocytosis
  3. Genome release: viral +RNA released from the endosome into the cytoplasm (pore-mediated penetration)
  4. Translation: the +RNA is translated (via an IRES, internal ribosome entry site) into one large polyprotein, which is cleaved into individual viral proteins
  5. Negative-strand synthesis: viral RNA replicase produces negative-stranded RNA (−RNA) using the +RNA as a template
  6. +RNA from −RNA: Host RNA polymerase makes new +RNA from the −RNA template
  7. Assembly: +RNAs are either translated to make more viral protein, or enclosed in capsids for packaging
  8. Lysis: lysis of the infected cell releases infectious progeny virions
Poliovirus replication summary: [ +RNA virion ] ↓ CD155 binding ↓ endocytosis ↓ +RNA released into cytoplasm ↓ IRES-mediated translation → polyprotein → cleaved viral proteins ↓ viral replicase: +RNA → −RNA ↓ host RNA pol: −RNA → +RNA (amplification) ↓ capsid assembly + packaging ↓ cell LYSIS → progeny virions
Why polio is FAST (vs. herpes' slow latency): +ssRNA is read directly as mRNA, replication is in the cytoplasm, and the cycle ends in lysis. No nucleus, no chromatin, no latency.

Two Polio Vaccines — And Why BOTH Are Needed HIGH YIELD

This is the most heavily flagged topic from Lecture 24. Know the names, mechanisms, advantages, and the trade-off that justifies keeping BOTH around.

The Two Vaccines

FeatureIPV (Inactivated Poliovirus Vaccine)OPV (Oral Polio Vaccine)
FormInactivated (killed) virusLive attenuated (weakened) virus
RouteInjection (shot)Oral (drops)
SafetySafe — cannot cause polioRarely (3 in 1,000,000 doses) can cause vaccine-associated paralytic poliomyelitis (VAPP)
US useCurrently in useNot authorized in the US since 2000; only used in outbreak settings
Spread / sheddingCannot spreadPatients can shed the attenuated virus → bystanders may pick up immunity
RiskMinimalAttenuated virus can mutate back to a dangerous form (VDPV — vaccine-derived poliovirus)

Why IPV Alone Isn't Enough HIGH YIELD

  • IPV is safe and reliably induces a strong antibody response that prevents paralytic disease
  • But IPV alone may not be enough in outbreak situations or in regions with low vaccination coverage
  • OPV's shed attenuated virus can spread and quietly confer protection on contacts (passive herd benefit) — useful in outbreaks where you need to immunize fast

Why OPV Alone Isn't Enough Either

  • OPV's attenuated virus can cause vaccine-associated paralytic polio (VAPP) — about 3 cases per million doses
  • If a population is largely unvaccinated, the attenuated virus can spread among them and accumulate mutations — eventually reverting to a dangerous (vaccine-derived) form
  • This is exactly why OPV is now used only in outbreak settings

The Compromise — Why Both Are Needed

  • IPV provides safe, reliable, individual-level protection — the routine choice
  • OPV provides faster spread of immunity in outbreak settings via shedding, and stronger gut immunity that limits transmission — held in reserve for outbreaks
  • You need IPV's safety AND OPV's transmission-blocking power to drive cases to zero globally

The Cautionary Tale: 2022 New Jersey Case

  • October 2022: the United States was confirmed as a country with circulating vaccine-derived poliovirus
  • The patient was UNVACCINATED
  • This is exactly the OPV-derived risk in action: somewhere in the world OPV virus was shed, mutated back toward virulence, and reached a person who had no IPV protection
  • Vaccination coverage among US kindergartners has dropped in pockets — e.g., WV at only 80.4-90.1% in 2020-2021
Two-line answer: IPV (shot, killed) is safe but doesn't transmit immunity. OPV (oral, attenuated) transmits immunity to contacts and gives strong gut immunity, but rarely causes VAPP and can revert to virulence in unvaccinated populations. Therefore: routine IPV + outbreak OPV is the global eradication strategy. Either alone has a fatal gap.

Common Themes

  • Both invade neurons — HSV the sensory neurons; polio the motor neurons
  • Both spread via the host's microtubule highway in some way (HSV uses dynein/kinesin directly)
  • Both produce most of their disease via host cell death — HSV causes lytic lesions in epithelium; polio kills lower motor neurons in the spinal cord

Three Mnemonic Trios

  • Herpes family: HSV-1, HSV-2, VZV, EBV, CMV (the 5 to know)
  • HSV entry: gB attaches, gD switches, gH/L fuses
  • HSV life cycle: Entry → Transit → Silencing → Reactivation (the four steps your professor explicitly listed)
  • Polio outcomes: 95 / 5 / 0.1 (asymptomatic / abortive / paralytic)
  • Polio vaccines: IPV (shot, safe) + OPV (oral, attenuated, transmits) — both needed
